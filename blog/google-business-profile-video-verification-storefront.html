Google Business Profile Video Verification for Storefront Businesses (Complete Step-by-Step Guide)

What to record, in what order, and why, based on five real video verifications completed for storefront businesses.

July 2026
Vinay Toshniwal

If you've been asked to complete Google Business Profile Video Verification, you're probably wondering exactly what Google expects to see in your video.

Many legitimate businesses fail verification, not because their business isn't genuine, but because their video doesn't clearly demonstrate the information Google needs to verify.

To better understand the process, I intentionally completed five Google Business Profile video verifications for different storefront businesses and carefully observed what worked consistently. Based on those real-world tests and my experience helping businesses with Google Business Profile verification and reinstatement, I've created this guide to help you maximize your chances of passing video verification.

Important: This guide is specifically for storefront businesses where customers visit your physical location. If you operate a Service Area Business (SAB), the verification process is different and is covered in a separate guide.

If you prefer watching the process in action, the video below walks through every step. This article expands on those steps with additional explanations, tips, and best practices.

What Is Google Business Profile Video Verification?

Google Business Profile Video Verification is a method Google uses to confirm that a business is genuine and that the person managing the profile is authorized to represent it.

Unlike traditional verification methods such as postcards or phone calls, video verification allows Google to review visual evidence of your business, its location, and your access to it.

Although many people think Google expects a perfect video, the reality is much simpler. Google primarily wants to verify three things:

  • Your business operates from the claimed location.
  • Your business genuinely exists.
  • You're authorized to manage the business.

Once you understand these three objectives, recording the video becomes much easier.

Before You Record Your Video

Before pressing the Record button, spend a few minutes preparing. Proper preparation can make the recording smoother and help avoid common mistakes.

1. Make Sure Your Google Business Profile Complies with Google's Guidelines

No video can fix a profile that violates Google's policies. Before attempting verification, review your business information carefully:

  • Business name
  • Business category
  • Address
  • Phone number
  • Website
  • Opening hours

Everything should accurately represent your business.

2. Check Your Website

Although a website isn't always required, it's a strong trust signal. Make sure your website:

  • Clearly represents your business
  • Displays your business name consistently
  • Includes your contact information
  • Matches your Google Business Profile
  • Is connected to Google Search Console (recommended)

3. Prepare Your Business Documents

Keep important documents nearby before recording. Examples include:

  • Business licence
  • Registration certificate
  • Utility bill
  • Business invoices
  • Business cards
  • Tax registration
  • Branded stationery

The easier they are to access, the smoother your recording will be.

4. Prepare Your Business Premises

Take a quick walk through your business before recording. Ensure your:

  • Storefront sign is visible
  • Reception area is tidy
  • Products are displayed
  • Equipment is accessible
  • Business branding is clearly visible

This helps create a smooth, uninterrupted recording.

Before You Press Record

Here are a few recommendations that can improve your video.

Turn On Location Services

Enable GPS on your phone before you start.

Speak if You Want To

Speaking isn't required. However, explaining what you're showing can provide useful context. For example: "This is my business sign." "These are our invoices." "This is our billing software."

Avoid Showing People

Try to focus on your business rather than yourself or other people. Although this isn't mandatory, it helps keep the video clear and professional.

Record Slowly

Google allows approximately five minutes. Don't rush. Move slowly enough that Google can clearly read your signage and documents.

Step 1: Prove Your Business Location

The first objective is proving that your business genuinely operates from the location shown on your Google Business Profile. I recommend recording in the following order.

Show an Official Street Sign

Begin your recording by showing an official street sign. If your business isn't directly beside it, that's perfectly acceptable. Walk or drive towards your business after recording the sign.

Show a Nearby Business

Next, record a nearby business displaying both its business name and address. This provides Google with another location reference.

Walk Towards Your Business

Continue towards your business. Try not to skip this transition. It helps Google understand that your business is genuinely located in that area.

Show Your Door Number

If your building has a door number, record it clearly.

Show Your Business Signboard

Next, record your storefront sign. Make sure your business name is clearly visible. If your sign also includes your address, phone number or website, that's even better.

Congratulations, you've now completed the first objective: proving your business location.

Step 2: Prove Your Business Exists

Now it's time to show that your business genuinely operates as described. Walk inside your business and slowly record:

  • Reception
  • Products
  • Inventory
  • Equipment
  • Workstations
  • Customer areas
  • Storage
  • Workshop
  • Office

Whenever possible, prioritize branded items. Examples include:

  • Business licence
  • Registration certificate
  • Utility bill
  • Business invoices
  • Company vehicle
  • Branded equipment
  • Uniforms
  • Business cards
  • Product packaging

Avoid spending too much time showing unrelated objects that don't help verify your business.

Step 3: Prove You're Authorized

Finally, Google wants to know that you're genuinely authorized to manage the business. The easiest way to demonstrate this is by performing actions that only the owner or an authorized employee can perform. Examples include:

  • Unlock the main entrance
  • Unlock the office
  • Unlock the workshop
  • Open the cash counter
  • Turn on the business computer
  • Open billing software
  • Create a sample invoice
  • Access internal business systems

These actions demonstrate legitimate access to the business. Once you've completed these actions, you've covered the third verification objective.

Review Before Submitting

Before uploading your recording, watch it carefully. Ask yourself:

  • Is everything clearly visible?
  • Did I record steadily?
  • Did I cover all three objectives?
  • Is anything blurry?
  • Did I accidentally skip something?

If necessary, record it again. Taking a few extra minutes now can save days of waiting later.

Common Mistakes That Can Cause Verification Failure

Many unsuccessful verifications happen because of avoidable mistakes. Common issues include:

  • Recording too quickly.
  • Forgetting to show the street sign.
  • Missing the storefront sign.
  • Poor lighting.
  • Blurry footage.
  • Showing unrelated items.
  • Not demonstrating authorization.
  • Shaky camera movement.
  • Recording away from the business location.
  • Attempting verification with a profile that doesn't comply with Google's guidelines.

Avoiding these mistakes can significantly improve the quality of your submission.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I pause the recording?

No. Business Video Verification must be recorded as one continuous video.

Do I have to speak?

No. Speaking is optional, but it can provide useful context.

Can I upload a pre-recorded video?

No. Google requires the recording to be created during the verification process.

How long can the recording be?

Google currently allows approximately five minutes.

Can another employee record the video?

Yes, provided they are genuinely authorized to manage the business and can demonstrate that authorization during the recording.

What if my verification fails?

Review your video, identify any missing evidence, and ensure your Google Business Profile fully complies with Google's guidelines before trying again if another verification opportunity is available.

Final Thoughts

Google Business Profile Video Verification isn't about producing a perfect video. It's about clearly demonstrating three simple things:

  • Your business operates from the claimed location.
  • Your business genuinely exists.
  • You're authorized to manage it.

If you plan your recording, move slowly, and provide clear visual evidence, you'll give your business the best possible chance of passing verification.
